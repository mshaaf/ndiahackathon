# Friendly Filter Plus — Complete Build Plan

## 1. What We Are Building

Friendly Filter Plus is a local, simulation-only decision-support dashboard for the Counter-UAS track.

It combines aircraft, drone, sensor, airfield, and simulated response-resource data to:

1. Protect friendly and civilian aircraft.
2. Identify likely hostile drones without treating missing information as proof of hostility.
3. Block unsafe response options.
4. Generate up to three safe courses of action in seconds.
5. Show ATC and ADOC how their decisions affect each other.
6. Continue operating safely when network data is delayed, missing, or contradictory.
7. Explain every recommendation in plain English.

Core pitch:

> Friendly Filter Plus turns mixed, imperfect airspace data into explainable, safety-screened simulation plans so ATC and ADOC can compare options in seconds without mistaking missing data for hostile intent.

This is not a replacement for fielded systems such as Anduril Lattice or FAAD C2. The hackathon contribution is a transparent, interoperable safety and COA-evaluation layer built around the event’s synthetic scenario.

### Non-negotiable boundaries

- No real weapon control or real-world targeting.
- “Effectors” are abstract simulated response resources supplied by the scenario.
- No external actuation endpoint.
- Ground truth remains hidden from the decision engine and is used only after a run to score results.
- Missing ADS-B or identification data never makes something hostile.
- Unknown, conflicting, friendly, civilian, and stale tracks cannot receive automated resource assignments.
- The Flipper Zero is excluded from the core build. Its radios do not provide the RF measurements used by DroneRF, and forcing it into the demonstration would be a gimmick rather than mission value.
- The judged demonstration must run locally without internet, cloud services, APIs, or a GPU.

### Why every part helps

| Project step | What it does | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Normalize incoming data | Converts different formats, times, coordinates, and units into one format | Prevents bad recommendations caused by mismatched data |
| Match cooperative aircraft | Compares sponsor/UDL identities and ADS-B tracks with sensor tracks | Protects Blue and civilian aircraft |
| Add RF and motion evidence | Uses DroneRF results and trajectory behavior as supporting evidence | Helps distinguish likely drones from ordinary or uncertain tracks |
| Preserve uncertainty | Marks weak or contradictory evidence as unknown | Prevents false confidence and simulated fratricide |
| Build moving safety zones | Predicts where protected aircraft will be during each response window | Blocks options that may intersect Blue or civilian movement |
| Generate several COAs | Compares speed, expected effectiveness, and resource use | Gives humans choices instead of one unexplained answer |
| Connect ATC and ADOC | Recalculates COAs when ATC chooses launch, hold, taxi, or reroute | Demonstrates the central coordination problem |
| Inject network faults | Delays or drops scenario messages | Proves the system remains safe outside a perfect network |
| Measure results | Compares Friendly Filter Plus against a simple baseline | Gives judges evidence that it works |
| Export results | Publishes tracks, advisories, and COAs as JSON and CoT | Supports the interoperability bonus |

## 2. SPARC Specification

### Functional requirements

- **FR1 — Scenario replay:** Load, pause, reset, and deterministically replay sponsor or locally prepared scenarios.
- **FR2 — Data ingestion:** Accept sponsor scenario records, ADS-B records, UDL/ARQ records when available, RF results, trajectory samples, and resource status.
- **FR3 — Normalization:** Convert timestamps to UTC, external positions to WGS84, and calculations to a local east/north/up coordinate system in meters.
- **FR4 — Track assessment:** Produce one assessed track containing classification, confidence, evidence, staleness, and predicted movement.
- **FR5 — Protected-aircraft filtering:** Identify Blue and civilian tracks using affirmative identity and space-time matching.
- **FR6 — Safety enforcement:** Reject every candidate response that conflicts with protected airspace, stale data, unavailable resources, or scenario rules.
- **FR7 — COA generation:** Return up to three distinct feasible plans: Balanced, Fastest Safe, and Conserve Resources. If no safe plan exists, clearly return `NO SAFE COA`.
- **FR8 — ATC/ADOC coordination:** Recalculate plans after ATC selects `CONTINUE`, `HOLD`, `TAXI_CLEAR`, or `REROUTE`.
- **FR9 — Explainability:** Show why a track was classified, why a COA scored well, and why alternatives were rejected.
- **FR10 — Degraded-network demonstration:** Reproduce latency, packet loss, feed outage, duplicates, and out-of-order messages.
- **FR11 — Evaluation:** Compute simulated Red stopped, protected entities selected or lost, wasted resource uses, unresolved tracks, and decision time.
- **FR12 — Interoperability:** Import documented JSON observations and export assessed tracks, airspace advisories, and COAs as JSON and Cursor-on-Target.
- **FR13 — Human approval:** Allow a user to approve a simulated plan while recording who approved it and which data version was visible.
- **FR14 — Auditability:** Save every scenario seed, input reference, rule version, classification explanation, candidate plan, rejection reason, and outcome.

### Classification rules

Every track receives one of these labels:

- `BLUE_PROTECTED`
- `CIVILIAN_PROTECTED`
- `LIKELY_RED`
- `UNKNOWN`
- `CONFLICTING`

Rules:

1. A valid sponsor/UDL Blue identity matched in location and time becomes `BLUE_PROTECTED`.
2. A valid civilian identity or ADS-B match becomes `CIVILIAN_PROTECTED`.
3. Missing ADS-B is not evidence of hostility.
4. `LIKELY_RED` requires a confidence of at least 0.80 and two independent positive evidence types, such as RF evidence plus inbound motion or sponsor sensor evidence.
5. Contradictory identification becomes `CONFLICTING`.
6. Everything else remains `UNKNOWN`.
7. Only `LIKELY_RED` tracks may enter COA generation.
8. Default matching tolerances are 250 meters horizontally, 100 meters vertically, and ±2 seconds. Sponsor-provided uncertainty values override these defaults.
9. A source becomes stale after five seconds or three expected update periods, whichever is longer. This remains configurable because event feeds may update at different rates.

### COA generation

Use OR-Tools CP-SAT with abstract scenario resources. Each resource has:

- Identifier and position
- Availability
- Allowed simulated range
- Time to effect
- Cooldown
- Capacity
- Synthetic success probability
- Scenario safety radius
- Sponsor-provided doctrine restrictions

Plan over a 30-second horizon in five-second slots.

Before optimization, apply hard constraints:

- No protected, unknown, conflicting, or stale target.
- No unavailable or stale resource.
- No candidate intersecting a predicted protected-aircraft safety volume.
- No violation of synthetic range, capacity, timing, cooldown, or sponsor rules.

After the hard safety gate:

- **Balanced:** Maximize expected weighted Red coverage; break ties by using fewer resources and responding sooner.
- **Fastest Safe:** Preserve at least 90% of Balanced’s expected coverage, then minimize response time.
- **Conserve Resources:** Preserve at least 80% of Balanced’s expected coverage, then minimize resource usage.
- Do not fabricate three alternatives when fewer than three genuinely different safe plans exist.

A simple baseline will assign the nearest eligible resource to the highest-confidence inbound track. Both systems use the same safety constraints so the comparison is fair.

### ATC coordination

For each ATC option, predict the protected aircraft’s future route volume and rerun the solver.

The demonstration’s visual centerpiece will be:

1. Friendly Filter Plus initially recommends Plan A.
2. ATC changes a Blue aircraft from `HOLD` to `TAXI_CLEAR`.
3. Its predicted path crosses Plan A’s safety area.
4. Plan A visibly becomes invalid.
5. The system generates a safer alternative in under two seconds and explains the change.

Dynamic routing will use a small sponsor-derived taxiway/waypoint graph with A* routing. It will not attempt certified 3D flight planning.

### Non-functional requirements

- Operate with 20–40 simultaneous tracks and 3–5 simulated resources.
- COA generation p95 latency below two seconds on a MacBook CPU.
- UI updates within 500 milliseconds of receiving new state.
- Deterministic output for the same scenario seed and configuration.
- No internet or GPU required during judging.
- No LLM in classification, safety enforcement, scoring, or optimization.
- At least 80% coverage for new backend decision logic.
- Accessible map symbols and labels; color cannot be the only indicator.
- Default server binding is `127.0.0.1`; LAN access requires an explicit option.
- Secrets come from environment variables and never enter logs or source control.
- Input size, schema, timestamp, enum, and coordinate validation at every ingestion boundary.

### Judging alignment

| Category | Demonstrated proof |
|---|---|
| Mission impact — 30% | More simulated Red stopped, zero protected assignments, fewer wasted actions, faster coordination |
| Technical execution — 25% | Data fusion, hard safety constraints, optimization, deterministic fault injection |
| Usability — 20% | One-screen map, three understandable choices, rejection explanations, clear network-health status |
| Security and sustainability — 15% | Local/offline operation, no decision-loop LLM, data lineage, secrets handling, documented interfaces |
| Team — 10% | Three stable owners, phase gates, continuous handoffs, three-speaker presentation |
| Interoperability bonus | JSON/CoT exports plus another team or second client consuming or contributing data |

The target and deliverables follow the live [Counter-UAS use case](https://hackathon.ndia.org/event/ndia-global-defense-hackathon-outstation-afcent/use-cases).

## 3. Dataset and Technical Plan

### Selected datasets

| Priority | Source | Exactly how it is used | How it helps | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sponsor Counter-UAS scenario package | Supplies base geometry, Red swarm plans, Blue/civilian movement, sensors, resource status, and scenario truth | Directly satisfies the challenge and powers the complete demo | Must be obtained and its handling rules confirmed |
| 2 | [ADS-B.lol](https://www.adsb.lol/docs/open-data/api/) | Save a public snapshot before judging; adapt aircraft identity, position, altitude, speed, heading, freshness, and quality fields | Adds a real cooperative-air data source and helps protect civilians | ADS-B is an assertion, can be stale or false, and is never sufficient by itself |
| 3 | [DroneRF](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/f4c2b4n755/1) | Train a small binary model for `drone_present` versus background; replay its probability as one synthetic sensor observation | Adds real RF-based drone evidence to the fusion pipeline | Only 227 segments and three drones; no live location; cannot support broad identification claims |
| 4 | [Drone Trajectory Data](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/shawnwuplus/drone-trajectory-data) | Normalize, translate, rotate, and resample recorded flight paths into the notional base | Provides realistic motion shapes and predicted positions for timing calculations | Contains no hostile/friendly labels; redistribution rights are unclear |
| 5 | Aerial Object Detection from the [event dataset catalog](https://hackathon.ndia.org/event/ndia-global-defense-hackathon-outstation-afcent/datasets) | Optional visual corroboration only | Could add a visible camera-confidence layer | Public catalog currently lacks enough schema, label, viewpoint, and license information |
| 6 | [MITRE Cursor-on-Target](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/qsDocDetails.aspx?ident_number=284928) | Encode assessed tracks and airspace advisories for export | Provides a recognized interoperability format | It is an interchange format, not a detection dataset |
| 7 | [BlueSTAQ UDL SDK](https://github.com/Bluestaq/udl-python-sdk) and ARQ | Optional live adapter for aircraft, air events, sensors, RF/radar observations, and generated scenarios | Makes the demo compatible with sponsor systems | Access and exact event permissions are currently unconfirmed |

### Dataset transformations

- **Sponsor package:** Convert all scenario entities into the internal schema. Keep scenario truth in a separate evaluator store that the planner cannot access.
- **ADS-B:** Use `hex`, `flight`, latitude, longitude, barometric/geometric altitude, groundspeed, track, vertical rate, quality indicators, `seen`, and `seen_pos`. Translate a saved public track into the notional scenario only when necessary, and record that transformation.
- **DroneRF:** Split training and testing by original recording segment—not random windows—to prevent data leakage. Extract approximately 64 log-power spectral bins plus energy, entropy, and peak-to-average features. Train a scikit-learn Random Forest and export the probability and model version.
- **Trajectory data:** Use the verified fields for date, time, latitude, longitude, altitude, gyro, acceleration, north/east/down position, orientation, wind speed, and wind direction. Motion paths may be transformed into the scenario, but must never be described as real attack traces.
- **CoT:** Export assessed location, identity, confidence, timestamp, staleness, and track category. JSON remains the primary two-way team interface.
- **UDL/ARQ:** Create an adapter behind the same internal observation contract. Failure or lack of credentials must automatically fall back to saved replay data.

### Aerial Object Detection decision gate

Include the vision module only if, during Phase 0:

- Files and annotations are available.
- A real drone class exists.
- Images represent a useful ground/surface detection viewpoint.
- Training and event use are permitted.
- A simple model can train and validate within 90 minutes.
- Validation reaches at least 0.50 mAP@0.5.

If any condition fails, remove vision completely. Do not replace it with HIT-UAV: that dataset contains downward-looking thermal images of people, cars, bicycles, and other vehicles—not images of drones being detected. This is confirmed by the [HIT-UAV paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03245).

### Architecture

```text
Sponsor replay / UDL / ADS-B / RF result / team JSON
                         ↓
              Validation + normalization
                         ↓
        Assessed tracks + evidence + staleness
                         ↓
     Protected routes and moving safety volumes
                         ↓
              Hard safety constraint gate
                         ↓
         COA optimizer + simple baseline engine
                         ↓
 Dashboard / metrics / audit / JSON and CoT exports
```

Minimum stack:

- Python 3.11
- FastAPI and Pydantic
- Pandas
- scikit-learn for the DroneRF model
- OR-Tools CP-SAT
- Shapely and pyproj
- SQLite
- React, TypeScript, Vite, and MapLibre GL JS
- WebSocket updates between backend and dashboard

Do not add a database server, message broker, Kubernetes, authentication platform, 3D rendering engine, or microservices.

### Public interfaces

Core records use schema version `1.0`, UUID identifiers, ISO-8601 UTC timestamps, WGS84 externally, and local east/north/up meters internally.

- `Observation`: source, modality, position, velocity, timestamp, receipt time, source identity, confidence, uncertainty, raw-record reference.
- `AssessedTrack`: category, probability, predicted path, staleness, matching evidence, conflicting evidence, explanation.
- `ResourceStatus`: abstract capability, position, availability, capacity, timing, and sponsor rules.
- `SafetyVolume`: protected entity, predicted time window, geometry, uncertainty buffer.
- `CourseOfAction`: ATC option, simulated assignments, timing, predicted metrics, rejection reasons, and rank.
- `EvidencePacket`: source references, transformations, confidence, rule/model version, timestamps, and human-review status.

Endpoints:

- `POST /api/v1/observations`
- `GET /api/v1/scenarios`
- `POST /api/v1/scenarios/{id}/start`
- `PUT /api/v1/fault-profile`
- `GET /api/v1/tracks`
- `POST /api/v1/coas:generate`
- `POST /api/v1/coas/{id}:approve`
- `GET /api/v1/metrics`
- `GET /api/v1/export?format=json|cot`
- `WS /api/v1/stream`

There will be no endpoint that executes a real response.

## 4. Phased Three-Person Build

Stable ownership:

- **Person A — Data and interoperability:** datasets, replay, adapters, RF preprocessing, UDL/CoT, provenance.
- **Person B — Decision engine:** classification rules, safety volumes, optimizer, baseline, evaluation.
- **Person C — User experience and integration:** dashboard, map, ATC interactions, network-health display, pitch and demo flow.

All three are responsible for integration tests and the final presentation.

| Phase | Time | Person A | Person B | Person C | Exit gate |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Permission and data gate | 0–1h | Obtain sponsor package; record licenses and permitted environments; test UDL credentials | Inspect scenario resource and doctrine fields | Convert the problem into the one-screen demo storyboard | Development pathway approved; Aerial dataset accepted or cut; `RIGHTS.md` started |
| 1. Contracts and golden scenario | 1–3h | Build one normalized scenario fixture | Define records, safety rules, configuration, and score outputs | Build dashboard shell and map | One scenario streams end-to-end using frozen version-1 contracts |
| 2. Replay and track assessment | 3–7h | Implement deterministic replay, ADS-B/trajectory adapters, and DroneRF preparation | Implement matching, classifications, evidence, prediction, and staleness | Display tracks, evidence, uncertainty, and protected routes | Evidence toggles visibly change track assessments; safety tests pass |
| 3. COA engine | 7–11h | Adapt sponsor resource data and create ground-truth evaluator | Build hard constraints, CP-SAT profiles, baseline, and explanations | Build COA cards and rejection drawer | Safe COAs or explicit `NO SAFE COA`; protected-track assignment test remains zero |
| 4. ATC/ADOC coordination | 11–14h | Add scenario event timing | Add ATC future routes, safety volumes, and simple A* taxi reroute | Add ATC controls and animated recomputation | Blue movement invalidates an unsafe plan and produces a new result in under two seconds |
| 5. Resilience and interoperability | 14–17h | Add latency/drop/outage replay and JSON/CoT export; add UDL adapter only if ready | Enforce stale-data behavior and tune solver performance | Add fault controls, data-health status, and stale warnings | System stays safe at 40% loss and during a feed outage; second client consumes output |

| 7. SPARC Refinement | 20–22h | Review adapters, licenses, and reproducibility | Review traceability, tests, performance, and safety invariants | Review usability, accessibility, and pitch clarity | Acceptance matrix passes; no critical/high finding; feature freeze |
| 8. Completion and rehearsal | 22–24h | Prepare saved replay and offline package | Run clean-machine regression and verify deterministic output | Lead two timed rehearsals and prepare screenshots/video fallback | Complete ten-minute presentation works twice without live internet |

Each owner updates one shared `docs/BUILD_BOOK.md` entry containing:

1. What changed.
2. How it helps the mission.
3. Inputs and outputs.
4. How to run and test it.
5. Exact demo clicks.
6. Known limitations.
7. The next owner and next concrete task.

Supporting documents are limited to:

- `README.md`
- `docs/BUILD_BOOK.md`
- `docs/DATA_CARD.md`
- `docs/PITCH.md`
- `RIGHTS.md`

Contracts freeze after Phase 1. Any contract change must be written in the build book and immediately communicated to the other two members.

### Cut order if time slips

Cut these in order:

1. Aerial vision module.
2. Live UDL connection; keep the replay adapter.
3. Inbound CoT support; keep JSON input and CoT export.
4. Visual animation polish.
5. Alternate reroutes beyond one simple taxi route.

Never cut the sponsor scenario, protected-track rules, safety constraints, coupled ATC decision, COA comparison, degraded-network demonstration, metrics, or local replay.

## 5. Refinement, Testing, and Presentation

### SPARC Refinement criteria

SPARC Refinement requires working code, so it occurs in Phases 7–8 rather than being falsely declared complete before implementation. The refinement pass must verify:

- Every functional requirement maps to at least one automated or scripted test.
- The planner never sees scenario ground truth.
- Every result can be reproduced from a scenario seed.
- No protected, unknown, conflicting, or stale track can receive an assignment.
- Every rejected plan includes a human-readable reason.
- Every dataset and model has source, license, transformation, and limitation records.
- No credentials, raw sponsor data, screenshots, prompts, or logs are sent to an external AI, cloud deployment, personal repository, or account without authorization.
- The demonstration launches from a fresh machine and continues with the network disabled.

### Acceptance scenarios

1. A valid Blue identity and matching position always produces `BLUE_PROTECTED`.
2. A civilian ADS-B track remains protected even when another sensor reports uncertainty.
3. Removing ADS-B never changes a track to `LIKELY_RED` by itself.
4. Conflicting identity evidence produces `CONFLICTING` and blocks assignment.
5. The engine returns up to three genuinely different safe COAs or clearly explains why fewer exist.
6. Changing a Blue aircraft from hold to taxi invalidates intersecting candidates and recomputes within two seconds.
7. Five-second-old critical data is visibly marked stale and cannot support a new assignment.
8. Duplicate and out-of-order messages do not rewind the state.
9. At 20% and 40% packet loss, the UI remains usable and safety constraints remain active.
10. A complete feed outage produces a safe degraded state rather than confident recommendations.
11. JSON import/export round-trips without losing required fields.
12. CoT export contains correct identity, location, time, category, and staleness.
13. Restarting the same seeded replay produces identical COAs and metrics.
14. The complete presentation works from saved local data with no internet.

### Benchmark target

Across 20 deterministic scenarios:

- Zero protected, civilian, unknown, or conflicting assignments.
- Balanced COA stops at least as many simulated Red threats as the baseline in at least 16 scenarios.
- Balanced COA uses no more wasted simulated actions than the baseline in at least 16 scenarios.
- COA generation p95 remains below two seconds.
- Every result contains an evidence and rejection trail.

If those targets are not met, the dashboard must not claim improvement.

### Ten-minute presentation

- **0:00–0:45 — Problem:** One person cannot safely coordinate a fast swarm, friendly aircraft, civilian traffic, and limited resources.
- **0:45–1:30 — Solution:** Explain Friendly Filter Plus in one sentence and show the dashboard.
- **1:30–2:15 — Honest differentiation:** It does not replace fielded C2; it makes safety logic, uncertainty, and ATC/ADOC tradeoffs visible and testable.
- **2:15–3:00 — Data:** Show exactly where sponsor data, ADS-B, DroneRF, trajectory data, and UDL/CoT enter.
- **3:00–6:30 — Main demonstration:** Run the swarm, show three COAs, change the Blue aircraft’s taxi decision, and show the unsafe plan being rejected and replaced.
- **6:30–7:30 — Degraded network:** Inject packet loss and a feed outage; show stale warnings and safe blocking.
- **7:30–8:30 — Evidence:** Compare results with the baseline.
- **8:30–9:15 — Interoperability:** Export a track/advisory and show another client consuming it.
- **9:15–10:00 — Close:** Map the results directly to mission impact, technical execution, usability, security, teamwork, and interoperability.

Speaker split:

- Person C: problem, interface, and ATC action.
- Person B: safety rules, optimizer, and metrics.
- Person A: datasets, degraded conditions, and interoperability.

### Assumptions and deployment policy

- The primary deployment is local on one MacBook, with the other computers connecting through browsers on the approved local network.
- Public cloud deployment is optional and may contain only sanitized synthetic/public data after organizer approval.
- Sponsor-controlled or government-controlled data will not be uploaded to Replit, AWS, external AI services, GitHub, or public hosting without explicit authorization.
- The team must confirm its approved development pathway before substantive coding; the event [terms document](https://hackathon.ndia.org/api/events/6/legal-document/terms/?v=1.1) makes this a Phase 0 requirement.
- UDL/ARQ access is an enhancement, not a dependency.
- DroneRF model training uses only the public CC BY 4.0 dataset unless the event explicitly authorizes training on event data.
- The unresolved Aerial Object Detection source is governed by the pass/fail gate above.
- Saved replay data is the judged source of truth, ensuring the demonstration works even when live services fail.
